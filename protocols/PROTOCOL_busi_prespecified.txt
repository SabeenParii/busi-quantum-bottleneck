PRESPECIFIED PROTOCOL
Quantum, product-state and quantum-inspired bottlenecks for breast ultrasound lesion segmentation:
qubit count, limited training data and a bottleneck audit (BUSI, with external testing on BUS-BRA and BrEaST)

Version 1.1 (14 November 2026). Version 1.0 was written before any image of BUSI, BUS-BRA or BrEaST was loaded
by the study code and before any training run; version 1.1 changes only the number of seeds and the run order
(see Deviations) and was committed before the first main run. The file is committed to the project repository; the commit time stamp is the time of
prespecification. Changes after that commit are recorded under "Deviations" with date and reason.

Target venue: 3rd IEEE International Conference on Cognitive Computing in Engineering, Communications,
Sciences and Biomedical Health Informatics (IC3ECSBHI 2027), full-paper deadline 31 December 2026.

Related work by the same group (cited as submitted manuscripts, no results reused):
  [P1] parameter-matched ablation of a quantum bottleneck for breast-region segmentation (BMC Medical Imaging);
  [P2] global and patch-wise quantum bottleneck for mammographic mass segmentation (Quantum Machine Intelligence).
This study uses new data (breast ultrasound), new controls (product-state circuit, full Fourier surrogate),
qubit counts 2, 4 and 8, limited-data training and external ultrasound test sets. No image, run or result of
P1 or P2 is used.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. QUESTIONS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1 (entanglement). In a U-Net bottleneck with trained block weights, does the 4-qubit circuit with its
    CNOT ring segment breast ultrasound lesions better than the same circuit without CNOT gates
    (product-state circuit)?
Q2 (function class). Does the circuit differ from a classical layer that spans its whole Fourier series
    (3^n terms), from a trigonometric layer that spans only its single-input terms, and from Dense(n)?
Q3 (efficiency, positive contribution). Are the 4-dimensional circuit and quantum-inspired trigonometric
    bottlenecks non-inferior, within 0.02 Dice, to the same network without compression and to a standard
    U-Net, with far fewer bottleneck parameters?
Q4 (limited data, positive contribution). With 10%, 25% and 50% of the training images, do the circuit or
    the trigonometric bottleneck segment better than Dense(n) or the uncompressed network?
Q5 (qubit count). How do the contrasts of Q1 and Q2 change with 2, 4 and 8 qubits?
Q6 (bottleneck audit, positive contribution). Does each network use its bottleneck code, how far do the
    block weights move, how entangled are the circuit states, and how well does a classical surrogate fitted
    to the trained circuit reproduce it? The audit is reported as a reusable procedure.

All estimands below are reported in full whatever their direction. A "positive contribution" is a claim
defined in advance (Section 8) and made only if its criterion is met.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. DATA
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 BUSI (development and internal testing). Dataset of Breast Ultrasound Images (Al-Dhabyani et al., Data in
    Brief 2020;28:104863): 780 PNG images (normal, benign, malignant) with lesion masks, Baheya Hospital, Cairo.
    Images are used as distributed; they are not redistributed by this project (only file names, split
    assignments and per-image scores are shared).
    Cleaning, fixed before loading (Pawlowska et al., Data in Brief 2023, letter on BUSI, with its
    supplementary list, Mendeley Data doi:10.17632/k8t3gnx9h6.1, CC BY 4.0):
      (a) duplicates and near-duplicates listed in the supplementary file form one group; one image per
          group is kept (the one with the lowest class-folder index, i.e. the first file name in sorted
          order); a group whose members appear in more than one class is removed entirely;
      (b) images listed as showing the axilla instead of the breast, a biopsy needle or a questionable
          lesion class are removed;
      (c) images with annotations (calipers, text) are kept; a sensitivity analysis removes those listed
          with annotations in the region of interest;
      (d) the normal class has no lesion and is not used for training; cleaned normal images are used only
          for the false-positive analysis (Section 7.6).
    Independent duplicate check (audit, does not change the cleaning): 64-bit difference hash of each image
    after resizing to 9x8; pairs with Hamming distance <= 4 that are not in the list are reported; if any
    such pair crosses folds, both images are placed in the fold of the lower-index image (recorded).
    If the supplementary list cannot be obtained, the hash check (Hamming <= 4) defines the duplicate groups
    and step (b) is skipped (recorded as a deviation).
    Masks: all mask files of an image (_mask, _mask_1, ...) are combined by union and binarized at 128.
2.2 Folds. Five folds, stratified by class (benign, malignant), random assignment with seed 2027 at the level
    of the kept images (after duplicate grouping). For fold k: test = fold k, validation = fold (k+1) mod 5,
    training = the other three folds. Every image is tested once per seed.
2.3 Limited-data subsets. For each fold, nested stratified random subsets of the training images with 10%,
    25% and 50% of them (seed 2027; the 10% subset is contained in the 25% subset, which is contained in the
    50% subset). Validation and test images are unchanged.
2.4 External test sets (no training, no tuning, no threshold selection):
      (i)  BUS-BRA (Gomez-Flores et al., Medical Physics 2024; Zenodo doi:10.5281/zenodo.7730709, CC BY 4.0):
           breast ultrasound images of 1,064 patients from Brazil with lesion delineations; all images with a
           delineation are used; patients are the clusters.
      (ii) BrEaST-Lesions_USG (Pawlowska et al., Scientific Data 2024; TCIA, CC BY 4.0): 256 images from
           Poland with lesion masks; images without a lesion are excluded; patients are the clusters.
           Used only if the files can be downloaded with masks; otherwise omitted (recorded).
2.5 Preprocessing (all sets): convert to 8-bit grayscale; zero-pad to square; resize to 256 x 256 (bilinear
    for images, nearest for masks); scale intensities to [0, 1]. No cropping, no histogram equalization.
    Training augmentation: horizontal flip (p = 0.5), rotation uniform in +/-10 degrees, brightness and
    contrast factors uniform in [0.8, 1.2]. MD5 checksums of all prepared arrays are recorded.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. MODELS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Host network (all hybrid and control models): U-Net encoder with four convolutional blocks (32, 64, 128, 256
filters; two 3x3 convolutions with batch normalization and ReLU; dropout 0.1, 0.1, 0.2, 0.2), max pooling,
bottleneck block with 512 filters (dropout 0.3) at 16 x 16, and a mirrored decoder with skip connections and
a 1 x 1 sigmoid output. Global bottleneck slot (n = number of qubits):
    16x16x512 -> global average pooling -> Dense(n, tanh) -> BLOCK(n -> n) -> replace-code layer
              -> Dense(512, ReLU) -> tile to 16x16x512 -> decoder
The replace-code layer is the identity in training (used only by the audit, Section 6).
BLOCK options for n in {2, 4, 8}:
  Q   circuit: RY(pi c_j / 2) on qubit j; two layers of Rot(phi, theta, omega) on every qubit followed by a ring of
      CNOT gates (j -> j+1, last -> first); outputs <Z_j>. 6n weights (the final omega of every qubit cannot affect
      the output; 5n effective). Exact state-vector simulation in TensorFlow.
  Qp  product-state circuit: Q without CNOT gates (same weights, same initial values). Each output depends on
      one input only.
  S   Fourier surrogate: features prod_j f_j(c_j) with f_j in {1, cos(pi c_j/2), sin(pi c_j/2)} (all 3^n
      products) followed by a linear map to n outputs and tanh. Contains every function Q can compute up to the
      output tanh. Weights: n * 3^n + n (n = 2: 20; 4: 328; 8: 52,496).
  Tr  trigonometric layer: tanh(A cos(pi c / 2 + phi)), A in R^{n x n}, phi in R^n (n^2 + n weights). Spans only
      single-input terms plus mixing.
  Cl  Dense(n, tanh) (n^2 + n weights).
  Id  identity (no weights).
References: "full" = same host without the slot (bottleneck map passed on uncompressed); "unet" = standard U-Net
(64-1024 filters, transposed-convolution decoder).
Model names: q2, qp2, s2, tr2, cl2, id2, q4, ..., id8, full, unet.
Initialization: for a given seed, every model with the same layer names starts from identical initial weights in
all shared layers (encoder, bottleneck block, Dense(n), Dense(512), decoder, output), copied from the q-model of
that seed and n; Qp copies the circuit weights of Q; the check is stored before training (max absolute
difference must be 0). Circuit angles are initialized uniformly on [0, 2 pi); Tr phase uniformly on [0, 2 pi) and A
Glorot uniform; S and Cl Glorot uniform with zero bias.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. TRAINING
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Loss: binary cross-entropy + (1 - soft Dice). Adam (beta1 0.9, beta2 0.999, eps 1e-7), base learning rate 1e-4,
batch size 16, at most 200 epochs, early stopping on validation loss with patience 20 (minimum 30 epochs),
ReduceLROnPlateau on validation loss (factor 0.5, patience 8, minimum 1e-6); the weights of the epoch with the
lowest validation loss are restored and tested. Mixed precision off. Seeds set for Python, NumPy and TensorFlow;
operation determinism enabled where available.
Block learning rate. In P1 and P2 the 20 block weights barely moved at the shared rate of 1e-4. Here every block
weight w is stored as v = w / k and used as w = k v, which multiplies its effective learning rate by k. The factor
k in {1, 10, 100} is selected separately for each of Q, Qp, S, Tr and Cl and each n by the lowest best validation
loss in one selection run per factor (fold 0, seed 100, full training data, at most 80 epochs, same settings
otherwise). Seed 100 is not an estimation seed, so selection and estimation do not share a seed. The selected
factors are recorded before the main runs and also used for the limited-data runs.
Seeds (version 1.1): 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 in all three plans.
Run plan (main):
  A  n = 4, full data: q4, qp4, s4, tr4, cl4, id4, full, unet                 8 x 5 folds x 5 seeds = 200
  B  n = 2 and 8, full data: q, qp, s, tr, cl                                10 x 5 folds x 5 seeds = 250
  C  n = 4, fractions 10%, 25%, 50%: q4, tr4, cl4, full                     12 x 5 folds x 5 seeds = 300
  Selection runs: 5 blocks x 3 n x 3 factors = 45 (fold 0, seed 100, at most 80 epochs).
Run order and stopping rule (version 1.1): the main runs are made in two waves. Wave 1 = seeds 0, 1, 2 of plans A,
then B, then C; wave 2 = seeds 3, 4 of plans A, then B, then C. On 15 December 2026 (cutoff, fixed now and
independent of any result) the runs stop. For each plan, the analysis uses every seed that is complete for all
models, folds (and fractions) of that plan; an incomplete seed is not analysed and is reported as not run. If wave 1
of a plan is incomplete at the cutoff, the complete seeds of that plan are analysed and the shortfall is reported.
The selection runs give the time per epoch of every model; the projected GPU-hours per plan (time per epoch x 1.5
x the mean number of epochs of the selection runs) are recorded with the selected factors in plan_decision.json
before the first main run, for reporting only. Runs are distributed over Colab sessions with lock files; a run
that fails is restarted from scratch with the same seed; no run is discarded for its result.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. EVALUATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Threshold 0.5 on the sigmoid output. Per-image Dice (primary metric; an image with empty prediction and
non-empty mask has Dice 0), IoU, precision, recall, 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95, pixels at 256 x 256;
undefined for empty predictions, which are counted separately), empty-prediction rate. Trainable parameters,
bottleneck parameters, training time and inference time per image (batch 1, same GPU type) are recorded.
External sets: every run of plan A is evaluated on BUS-BRA and BrEaST.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. BOTTLENECK AUDIT (every run of plans A and B; the same procedure is reported as a reusable tool)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Code replacement: test Dice when the block output is replaced by (const) the mean code over the training
    images, or (swap) the code of a randomly drawn training image (10 fixed draws, averaged). Dice drop =
    normal - replaced. A model "uses its code" if the mean drop over runs is at least 0.02 Dice and its 95% CI
    excludes 0.
6.2 Weight movement: relative change ||w_final - w_init|| / ||w_init|| of the block, of Dense(n) and Dense(512);
    circuit angles wrapped to (-pi, pi].
6.3 Gradient share: norm of the loss gradient of the block weights relative to all weights, at the first and
    the restored epoch (4 fixed training batches).
6.4 Entanglement (Q models): mean Meyer-Wallach entanglement of the circuit states over the test codes (Qp has 0
    by construction).
6.5 Classical surrogate fit (Q models): a linear map on the 3^n Fourier features fitted by least squares to the
    trained circuit outputs on the training codes; R^2 on the test codes. R^2 near 1 means the trained circuit is
    reproduced exactly by a classical model (as the theory implies for exact simulation).
6.6 Finite shots (q4, plan A): test Dice when each <Z_j> is estimated from 1,024 shots (binomial draws, 5 repeats,
    qubits drawn independently).

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. STATISTICS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Unit: per-image test Dice. Uncertainty: crossed hierarchical bootstrap over clusters (BUSI: kept images, a
duplicate group counting as one; external sets: patients) and seeds, the same resampled clusters and seeds for both
models of a contrast; B = 10,000 replicates; numpy default_rng(0), one generator per family in the order listed.
Difference = mean over seeds and images of (A - B). 95% percentile intervals; two-sided bootstrap p-values; Holm
correction within each family. Equivalence: the 90% interval inside +/-0.02 Dice (two one-sided tests at 5%).
Non-inferiority: one-sided test of H0: difference <= -0.02, at 5% after Holm (equivalently, the lower 95% one-sided
limit, i.e. the lower limit of the 90% interval, above -0.02). Reported for every contrast: difference, 95% CI, 90%
CI, Holm p, verdict (difference, equivalent, non-inferior, inconclusive), the CI half-width as the smallest
difference the design could detect, and per-seed and leave-one-seed-out estimates.
7.1 F1 primary (BUSI, n = 4, full data): q4 - qp4, q4 - s4, q4 - tr4, q4 - cl4.
7.2 F2 efficiency (BUSI, non-inferiority, margin 0.02): tr4 - full, q4 - full, tr4 - unet, q4 - unet.
7.3 F3 limited data (BUSI, n = 4), one family per fraction: q4 - cl4, tr4 - cl4, q4 - full, tr4 - full.
7.4 F4 qubit count: q - qp and q - tr at n = 2 and n = 8 (four contrasts); model levels for all n (descriptive);
    the change of q - qp and q - tr between n = 2, 4 and 8 is descriptive.
7.5 Secondary, external sets: F1 and F2 contrasts on BUS-BRA and on BrEaST (separate families per set).
7.6 Secondary: on cleaned BUSI normal images, rate of non-empty predictions (false-positive images) per model, n = 4.
7.7 Sensitivity: F1 and F2 without the images listed with annotations in the region of interest; F1 by class
    (benign, malignant), descriptive.
Margin: +/-0.02 Dice, the margin of P2; for reference, the between-seed SD of model Dice is reported.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. PREDEFINED CLAIMS (made only if the criterion is met; otherwise the result is reported as found)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C1 Efficiency: "a 4-dimensional quantum-inspired (trigonometric) / circuit bottleneck with X trainable bottleneck
   weights is non-inferior to the uncompressed network (and to a standard U-Net)": F2 non-inferiority verdict.
C2 Limited data: "with p% of the training data the circuit / trigonometric bottleneck scores higher than Dense(4) /
   the uncompressed network": F3 difference with Holm p < 0.05 and positive direction.
C3 Entanglement: "the CNOT gates add (no) measurable benefit": F1 q4 - qp4 difference (positive) or equivalence.
C4 Function class: "a classical surrogate of the circuit's Fourier series matches the circuit": F1 q4 - s4
   equivalence and audit 6.5 R^2 >= 0.99.
C5 Audit: "the networks use (do not use) their bottleneck code": criterion of 6.1.
A claim of quantum advantage is made only if q4 scores higher than qp4, s4, tr4 and cl4 in F1 (all four with Holm
p < 0.05) and the direction holds on BUS-BRA.
